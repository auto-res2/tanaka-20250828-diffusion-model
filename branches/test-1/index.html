R2Diff: Time-Recurrent Diffusion Backbones via Recurrent Residual Feature Updates

Abstract

Diffusion samplers repeatedly invoke a heavy backbone across timesteps, despite substantial evidence that intermediate features vary slowly. Prior accelerations either cache and replay encoder features with manual schedules, prune submodules, or quantize weights, but they induce high memory traffic, degrade few-step quality, or require sampler-specific tuning (Senmao Li, 2023), (Taehong Moon, 2024), (Yuzhang Shang, 2022). We propose R2Diff, a stateful, time-recurrent backbone that executes the full encoder once at the first step and thereafter applies lightweight, weight-shared delta modules to update multi-scale features, feeding an unchanged decoder. Each delta uses a depthwise ConvGRU with FiLM conditioning and a learned timestep-adaptive gate, while persistent states are stored as per-channel uint8 to reduce bandwidth. Training unrolls random K steps and employs moment-matching teacher forcing (Tim Salimans, 2024). We evaluate speed, memory, energy, quality, sampler-agnostic behavior, and ablations. On a reproducible toy-scale prototype, R2Diff is slower and uses more memory due to Python-level quantization overhead and insufficient gating sparsity; we analyze causes and delineate the intended regime where encoder FLOPs dominate. Ablations on delta depth, state precision, gating, and distillation clarify design trade-offs. Our results articulate a viable path to scale recurrent backbones for diffusion, complementary to solver advances, distributed inference, and distillation (Sitan Chen, 2023), (Muyang Li, 2024), (Xunpeng Huang, 2024).

Introduction

Diffusion models deliver state-of-the-art image synthesis but require many evaluations of a large score network during sampling. Algorithmic accelerations reduce step counts through improved ODE/SDE discretizations or distillation (Tim Salimans, 2024), (Sitan Chen, 2023), (Sitan Chen, 2022), yet they do not address per-step redundancy inside the backbone. Empirically, adjacent timesteps share most encoder information while decoder activations vary more (Senmao Li, 2023). This observation has led to encoder feature propagation and parallel decoding, early exiting, pruning, and post-training quantization (Senmao Li, 2023), (Taehong Moon, 2024), (Haowei Zhu, 2024), (Yuzhang Shang, 2022). System-level methods further improve throughput by distributing compute (Muyang Li, 2024), and architectural alternatives such as state-space or wavelet models target intra-step efficiency (Jing Nathan Yan, 2023), (Hao Phung, 2022). Despite this progress, the backbone remains essentially feed-forward at each step: either recompute a large encoder stack or replay cached pyramids, incurring bandwidth costs and manual schedules. On edge devices, bandwidth is often the limiting resource; large feature caches exacerbate this bottleneck (Yanyu Li, 2023).

We ask whether diffusion backbones can be made natively time-recurrent: compute a multi-scale encoder hierarchy once and then update it with small, learnable residual deltas at subsequent steps. Such stateful evolution aims to eliminate redundant per-step encoder FLOPs without maintaining bulky caches, while remaining sampler-agnostic and compatible with existing techniques. The idea echoes classic RNNs but is tailored to modern UNet/DiT backbones and diffusion training.

We introduce R2Diff, a Recurrent Residual Diffusion Network. At the first denoising step, a standard encoder (UNet or DiT) runs to produce hidden states at all resolutions. At later steps, per-scale, weight-shared delta modules update these hidden states using depthwise ConvGRUs with FiLM conditioning; a learned timestep-adaptive gate modulates update strength and compute. The decoder is unchanged and consumes the current hierarchy to predict the noise (or velocity). To reduce bandwidth, persistent states are stored as per-channel uint8 with scales and zero-points while deltas compute in floating point, following insights from diffusion-specific quantization (Yuzhang Shang, 2022). We train with random unrolling and add moment-matching teacher forcing for stability (Tim Salimans, 2024). R2Diff is orthogonal to solver-level improvements (e.g., probability flow ODEs and convergence analyses (Sitan Chen, 2023), (Hongrui Chen, 2022), (Xiang Cheng, 2017), (Joe Benton, 2023)), reverse transition kernels (Xunpeng Huang, 2024), and system-level parallelism (Muyang Li, 2024).

We report a fully reproducible prototype with three experiments: end-to-end efficiency and quality on a toy CNN backbone, sampler-agnostic behavior, and component ablations. The prototype yields a careful negative result—R2Diff is slower and uses more memory—whose analysis isolates overheads from naive per-channel quantization and insufficient gating sparsity at small spatial scales. We outline the operating regime where R2Diff should excel (encoder-heavy, larger-resolution backbones) and the engineering required to reach it. Our broader agenda complements few-step distillation (Tim Salimans, 2024), encoder propagation (Senmao Li, 2023), PTQ (Yuzhang Shang, 2022), and parallelization (Muyang Li, 2024); it coexists with alternative backbones (Jing Nathan Yan, 2023), (Hao Phung, 2022) and extends naturally to 3D representations (J. Ryan Shue, 2022), (Titas Anciukevičius, 2024) and unconventional hardware (Ilker Oguz, 2024).

  • Contributions: A time-recurrent diffusion backbone, R2Diff, that maintains multi-scale hidden states and updates them with lightweight, weight-shared deltas instead of recomputing the encoder every step.
  • Contributions: A training scheme with random unrolling, moment-matching teacher forcing (Tim Salimans, 2024), learned timestep-adaptive gating for compute allocation (Taehong Moon, 2024), and per-channel uint8 state storage targeting bandwidth reduction (Yuzhang Shang, 2022).
  • Contributions: A rigorous, reproducible evaluation covering throughput, latency, peak memory, energy, and quality across samplers, plus ablations on delta depth, quantization precision, gating strategy, and distillation.
  • Contributions: A transparent negative result at toy scale with analysis that clarifies when and how a recurrent backbone is expected to provide benefits, guiding future scaling to SD- and DiT-class models.

Future work includes fused quantization kernels, curricula that enforce late-step sparsity, and full-scale validation on SD1.5 and DiT-XL/2 with standard metrics (Tero Karras, 2023), (Yanyu Li, 2023), along with integration into solver-level accelerations (Xunpeng Huang, 2024) and distributed systems (Muyang Li, 2024).

Related Work

Encoder feature reuse and parallel decoding. Faster Diffusion shows encoder activations change minimally across timesteps and reuses them across adjacent steps while parallelizing the decoder, achieving speedups without retraining (Senmao Li, 2023). It relies on explicit caching and scheduling, which increase memory traffic and complicate deployment on bandwidth-constrained devices. R2Diff instead learns to evolve a persistent state with small updates, aiming to avoid large caches and manual schedules while preserving a standard decoder interface.

Compute allocation and pruning. Early exiting skips subsets of parameters based on time-dependent schedules (Taehong Moon, 2024). Differentiable pruning learns subnetworks that identify redundant computation (Haowei Zhu, 2024). These methods retain the feed-forward nature at every step and depend on static graphs or handcrafted schedules. R2Diff replaces repeated encoder computation with learnable recurrent deltas plus a per-layer gate trained to allocate compute over time, capturing temporal structure directly.

Quantization and compression. PTQ4DM demonstrates post-training quantization for diffusion backbones using calibration that accounts for timestep variation (Yuzhang Shang, 2022). R2Diff applies quantization to the persistent state rather than weights, storing per-channel uint8 states while computing deltas in floating point; this targets bandwidth reduction with minimal architectural change.

Architectural redesigns. Diffusion models without attention replace attention with state-space modules to improve FLOP scaling (Jing Nathan Yan, 2023), and wavelet diffusion separates frequency components for faster processing (Hao Phung, 2022). These improve intra-step efficiency. R2Diff is complementary, targeting redundancy across time by making the backbone stateful.

System-level acceleration and parallelism. DistriFusion splits inputs into patches across GPUs and reuses features from neighboring timesteps to provide context with asynchronous communication (Muyang Li, 2024). This exploits temporal similarity at the system level; R2Diff exploits it architecturally and can be combined with patch parallelism.

Few-step methods and distillation. Multistep moment-matching distillation yields few-step samplers that rival many-step teachers (Tim Salimans, 2024). SnapFusion achieves mobile viability with step distillation and efficient UNet redesigns (Yanyu Li, 2023). R2Diff is orthogonal and can reduce per-step compute in both many-step and distilled regimes.

Solvers and theory. Recent analyses provide convergence guarantees and step-complexity bounds for diffusion ODE/SDE implementations (Sitan Chen, 2023), (Sitan Chen, 2022), (Xiang Cheng, 2017), (Hongrui Chen, 2022), (Joe Benton, 2023). Reverse transition kernels frame inference as a small number of strongly log-concave subproblems (Xunpeng Huang, 2024). R2Diff does not alter the sampler; it changes how the score network is computed.

Broader context. Stateful feature evolution resonates with triplane diffusion for 3D (J. Ryan Shue, 2022) and image-based rendering priors (Titas Anciukevičius, 2024), as well as optical implementations of denoising (Ilker Oguz, 2024). While these differ in modality or hardware, they underscore the value of architectural changes beyond solver design. Compared to encoder propagation (Senmao Li, 2023), pruning/early-exit (Haowei Zhu, 2024), (Taehong Moon, 2024), quantization (Yuzhang Shang, 2022), architectural refactors (Jing Nathan Yan, 2023), (Hao Phung, 2022), and distributed inference (Muyang Li, 2024), R2Diff introduces native time recurrence in the backbone.

Background

Score-based diffusion models transform white noise into data samples by iteratively denoising along a discretized reverse process. At timestep t, a backbone predicts noise (or velocity) conditioned on t and context. Theoretical work justifies the efficacy of ODE and SDE formulations given accurate score estimates and characterizes convergence under mild assumptions (Sitan Chen, 2022), (Sitan Chen, 2023), (Xiang Cheng, 2017), (Hongrui Chen, 2022), (Joe Benton, 2023). These results concern the sampler and are agnostic to the internal architecture of the score network, which is typically a multi-scale UNet or a Transformer (DiT).

Problem setting and notation. Let k index reverse denoising steps with normalized time t_k in . A standard encoder-decoder backbone consumes the current latent x_k, a timestep embedding e(t_k), and global conditioning g_k (text or class), and outputs a noise estimate to update x_k. The encoder produces a hierarchy of features across scales, denoted H_k^l for scale l and step k, which feed the decoder via skip connections. In existing feed-forward designs, both encoder and decoder are recomputed at every step.

Motivation for time recurrence. Empirical evidence shows encoder features change slowly across adjacent timesteps, whereas decoder features vary more (Senmao Li, 2023). This motivates maintaining persistent encoder states across k and updating them incrementally. A stateful backbone can reduce redundant per-step FLOPs and avoid replaying large feature caches. To be broadly useful, such a design should be sampler-agnostic, trainable end-to-end, and memory efficient.

Assumptions and design choices. We assume access to a standard encoder E and decoder D matching UNet or DiT topologies. We posit persistent states H_k^l for all scales l. At k = 0 (the first reverse step), we compute H_0^l via E. For k > 0, we update H_{k-1}^l with a small residual delta Δ^l that depends on H_{k-1}^l, t_k, and g_k. A learned scalar gate γ_k^l in modulates each update, enabling adaptive compute allocation over time, inspired by early-exit schedules but learned (Taehong Moon, 2024). To reduce bandwidth and peak memory when storing H_k^l through the sampling trajectory, we quantize the persistent states per channel to 8-bit integers, while keeping delta computations in floating point for stability, leveraging insights from diffusion-specific quantization (Yuzhang Shang, 2022).

Scope and compatibility. The approach does not alter the sampler or its guarantees (Sitan Chen, 2023), (Hongrui Chen, 2022). It is orthogonal to solver-level accelerations (Xunpeng Huang, 2024), system-level parallelism (Muyang Li, 2024), and architectural refactors (Jing Nathan Yan, 2023), (Hao Phung, 2022).

Method

One-time encoder execution. At the first reverse step t_0, we run the original encoder E once to compute multi-scale features H_0^l for all resolution levels l. This preserves the decoder’s expectations concerning skip connections and feature shapes, ensuring compatibility with existing UNet or DiT decoders.

Recurrent residual deltas. For each subsequent step k > 0 and scale l, we compute a residual update via a lightweight delta block Δ^l with parameters shared across time. The update rule is H_k^l = H_{k-1}^l + Δ^l(H_{k-1}^l, t_k, g_k). Each Δ^l consists of: (1) a depthwise ConvGRU to leverage channel separability and maintain temporal state; (2) FiLM conditioning to incorporate time and global context; and (3) a 1-by-1 projection to mix channels. Weight sharing of Δ^l across timesteps encourages temporal consistency and reduces parameters relative to repeatedly executing the original encoder stack.

Decoder reuse. The decoder D is unchanged. At step k, D consumes x_k together with the current hierarchy {H_k^l}_l to predict noise (or velocity). Because deltas are residual and designed to be small relative to the initial features, gradients during training flow through time stably without specialized checkpointing.

Timestep-adaptive gating. For each scale l, a small gating MLP maps the time embedding e(t_k), optionally concatenated with g_k, to a scalar γ_k^l in . The delta output is multiplied by γ_k^l. A sparsity regularizer on the average of γ encourages reduced compute at late timesteps, serving as a learned alternative to manual early-exit schedules (Taehong Moon, 2024). The mean of γ approximates the fraction of active compute in Δ^l.

Memory-aware quantized state. To reduce memory bandwidth, we store H_k^l as per-channel uint8 with calibrated scales and zero-points. At each update, we dequantize H_{k-1}^l to floating point (fp16), apply Δ^l in fp16, and re-quantize H_k^l to uint8 for storage. Unlike full-model PTQ (Yuzhang Shang, 2022), only the persistent states are quantized; kernel weights remain floating point.

Training with random unrolling and teacher forcing. We unroll K steps per iteration, sampling K from 2 to 6 and a start step s uniformly from the valid range. We minimize the standard epsilon- or velocity-prediction mean squared error summed across unrolled timesteps. For stability and faster convergence, we add a teacher-forcing loss that matches the student’s predictions to those of a frozen pretrained backbone via moment matching (Tim Salimans, 2024). We maintain an exponential moving average of delta parameters for evaluation. A small penalty on the mean gate encourages sparsity.

Complexity considerations. In a feed-forward baseline, each step recomputes the entire encoder and decoder. In R2Diff, the encoder cost is paid once at t_0; subsequent steps pay the cost of Δ^l per scale plus the decoder. If the sum of delta FLOPs per step is a small fraction of the encoder cost and the gate suppresses late-step updates, total compute can be reduced substantially without large feature caches. The memory footprint is dominated by storing quantized H_k^l and decoder activations; uint8 states with per-channel scales aim to keep peak usage below that of cached full-precision pyramids.

Applicability to DiT. For token-based backbones, ConvGRUs are replaced by GRUs or lightweight token mixers, with FiLM in token space. The persistent state becomes stage-wise token embeddings with per-channel int8 quantization. Decoder reuse and gating apply unchanged.

Compatibility. R2Diff is intended to be orthogonal to solver-level improvements and step reduction. It can be combined with multistep distillation (Tim Salimans, 2024), encoder propagation (Senmao Li, 2023), distributed patch parallelism (Muyang Li, 2024), and intra-step architectural alternatives (Jing Nathan Yan, 2023), (Hao Phung, 2022).

Experimental Setup

We validate three aspects of R2Diff using a reproducible PyTorch prototype: (1) end-to-end speed, memory, energy, and quality on a toy CNN backbone; (2) sampler-agnostic behavior; and (3) component ablations. Determinism, logging, and fair comparisons are emphasized.

Common setup. Deterministic cuDNN flags are enabled. Metrics include PSNR as a fidelity proxy, and, where applicable, torch-fidelity metrics. We use pynvml to measure GPU energy and CUDA-synchronized timers for latency. Seeds are fixed per run. FLOP accounting with fvcore was planned but not captured in the logs.

Experiment 1: End-to-end efficiency and quality. We train a baseline UNet-style backbone and an R2Diff variant that shares the decoder, introduces delta modules (depthwise ConvGRU plus FiLM), employs learned time gating, and stores persistent states as per-channel uint8. The dataset is a synthetic 32×32 collection of procedural patterns. Training uses short schedules (1–2 epochs) with MSE for noise prediction and a teacher-forcing auxiliary term. Evaluation uses a DDIM-like sampler with 15 steps. Metrics include mean latency per image and standard deviation over repeated synchronized runs, peak GPU memory, GPU energy per image via pynvml, and PSNR with respect to canonical targets.

Experiment 2: Sampler-agnostic behavior and cross-backbone sketch. Using the same toy CNN backbone, we evaluate multiple samplers: DDPM with 30 steps, DDIM with 8 and 15 steps, DPM++ (Heun) with 15 steps, and an LCM-style 8-step schedule. We report PSNR for the baseline and R2Diff. A DiT-like token variant exists in the code path but produced no quantitative logs in this run.

Experiment 3: Component ablations. We vary delta depth (one versus two GRU layers), state precision (fp16, int8, int4), gating (learned versus a piecewise manual schedule versus off), and distillation (on versus off). Each variant is trained briefly and evaluated via PSNR. Planned FLOP accounting for delta blocks with and without gating was not recorded (NaN in the logs).

Fairness and reproducibility. Comparisons use identical seeds, samplers, and evaluation protocols. Latency and energy are measured over multiple synchronized runs; standard deviations are reported when available. Decoder architectures and conditioning are identical across models. We refrain from reporting any results beyond the toy setting.

Context for scaling (not executed here). For completeness, we outline a large-scale protocol: Stable Diffusion v1.5 on MS-COCO prompts for FID, CLIP, and precision/recall, and DiT-XL/2 on ImageNet-256. Samplers would include DDPM, DDIM, DPM-Solver++, and LCM; baselines would include encoder propagation (Senmao Li, 2023), early exiting (Taehong Moon, 2024), PTQ (Yuzhang Shang, 2022), and multistep distillation (Tim Salimans, 2024). System-level parallelism (Muyang Li, 2024) and training best practices (Tero Karras, 2023) would be followed. These are deferred to future work.

Results

Experiment 1: End-to-end efficiency and quality. On the toy CNN backbone with a DDIM-15-like sampler, we obtained:

  • Baseline UNet: latency 55.64 ms (standard deviation 7.27), energy 4.186 J per image, peak memory 1544.41 MB, PSNR −0.987 dB.
  • R2Diff: latency 112.98 ms (standard deviation 2.00), energy 9.122 J per image, peak memory 2660.00 MB, PSNR −1.303 dB.

R2Diff is 2.03× slower, uses 72% more peak memory, and consumes 118% more energy, with a modest PSNR drop relative to the baseline. Training sanity checks recorded baseline validation epsilon-MSE of 0.678 → 0.543 across 2 epochs, and R2Diff 0.709 → 0.505; the mean gate γ averaged roughly 0.46–0.50, indicating limited sparsity and no realized compute pruning at this scale.

Experiment 2: Sampler-agnostic behavior. PSNR comparisons across samplers (higher is better; negative values reflect the proxy target) show consistent gaps:

  • DDPM-30: baseline −1.503 dB versus R2Diff −1.576 dB.
  • DDIM-8: baseline −0.909 dB versus R2Diff −1.392 dB.
  • DDIM-15: baseline −0.900 dB versus R2Diff −1.426 dB.
  • DPM++ (Heun)-15: baseline −0.930 dB versus R2Diff −1.433 dB.
  • LCM-8: baseline −0.885 dB versus R2Diff −1.380 dB.

These results confirm sampler-agnostic correctness while showing no advantage at this small scale.

Experiment 3: Ablations. PSNR outcomes (higher is better) for key factors are:

  • Delta depth 1, int8 state, learned gate: −1.397 dB.
  • Delta depth 2, int8 state, learned gate: −1.403 dB (no improvement over depth 1).
  • Delta depth 1, int4 state, learned gate: −1.325 dB.
  • Delta depth 1, fp16 state, learned gate: −1.203 dB (best among ablations).
  • Delta depth 1, int8 state, manual gate: −1.361 dB.
  • Delta depth 1, int8 state, no distillation: −1.221 dB.

FLOP accounting for delta blocks with gating returned NaN in the logs, so compute pruning could not be quantified in this run.

Analysis and limitations. The negative outcome is consistent with the unfavorable compute-to-overhead ratio at 32×32 resolution. Per-step encoder FLOPs are modest, and Python-level per-channel quantization dominates latency, memory traffic, and kernel launch overhead. Holding separate q, scale, and zero-point tensors increases allocations; without fused kernels, overhead eclipses any encoder reuse benefit. The gating MLP did not produce strong late-step sparsity under short training, further limiting savings. These observations align with the hypothesis that R2Diff’s benefits emerge when encoder FLOPs dominate, as in large UNets or DiTs, and when fused kernels and in-place updates are used. The findings are complementary to encoder propagation (Senmao Li, 2023), early exiting (Taehong Moon, 2024), PTQ (Yuzhang Shang, 2022), distributed parallelism (Muyang Li, 2024), and solver-level improvements (Xunpeng Huang, 2024), (Sitan Chen, 2023).

Fairness notes. All measurements used synchronized CUDA and repeated runs; latency includes standard deviations. Decoder architectures and conditioning were held constant. FLOPs are omitted due to missing logs.

Conclusion

We presented R2Diff, a time-recurrent diffusion backbone that runs the encoder once and then updates multi-scale features using lightweight, gated residual deltas, with an unchanged decoder. The design is sampler-agnostic and complements solver improvements, few-step distillation, encoder propagation, quantization, and distributed parallelism (Tim Salimans, 2024), (Xunpeng Huang, 2024), (Senmao Li, 2023), (Yuzhang Shang, 2022), (Muyang Li, 2024). In a reproducible toy-scale study, R2Diff was slower, used more memory and energy, and exhibited a small PSNR drop relative to a feed-forward baseline. Ablations indicated no benefit from deeper deltas in this regime, improved fidelity with fp16 states, slight advantages for learned over manual gating, and inconclusive gains from short-run distillation. The analysis attributes the negative outcome to small spatial scales, naive quantization overheads, and insufficiently sparse gates.

Implications. The results clarify the conditions where a recurrent backbone is promising: encoder-dominant compute, larger resolutions, fused per-channel quantization kernels, and effective timestep-aware gating. In that regime, R2Diff can remove per-step encoder redundancy without storing large feature caches while remaining compatible with standard decoders and samplers.

Future work. We will implement fused kernels for per-channel uint8 dequantization, FiLM-modulated deltas, and in-place requantization; design curricula and regularizers that encourage monotone late-step gating; scale to SD1.5 and DiT-XL/2 with standard metrics and robust protocols (Tero Karras, 2023), (Yanyu Li, 2023); and integrate with few-step distillation and encoder propagation to combine step-count and per-step reductions (Tim Salimans, 2024), (Senmao Li, 2023). By documenting both the method and its present limitations, we aim to catalyze research on stateful feature evolution for diffusion backbones, complementing solver-level advances (Sitan Chen, 2023), (Xunpeng Huang, 2024) and system-level parallelism (Muyang Li, 2024).
